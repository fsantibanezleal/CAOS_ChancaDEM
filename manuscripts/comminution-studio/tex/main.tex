\begin{abstract}
\noindent A cone crusher turns a feed into a product whose size, throughput and power are set by four levers: the
closed-side setting, the eccentric throw, the eccentric speed and the feed size distribution. In Chilean copper mining
the concentrator, where crushing and grinding sit, took $55.7\%$ of the sector's electricity in 2024, so the setting of a
crusher is an energy decision. ChancaDEM is an open studio that couples a Whiten population balance with an Andersen and
Napier-Munn classification, a JKMRC $t_{10}$ breakage function, a reduced Evertsson capacity model, Bond's power law and
Morrell's size-specific energy in one engine written in Python and in TypeScript, runs it in the browser, and holds it
against ten measured surveys of a Metso HP500 secondary cone crusher on itabirite iron ore (Rocha et al., 2024, CC BY).
Every fitted quantity is refit without the held-out survey (strict leave-one-survey-out), with a constant-mean baseline,
a label-shuffle control and a nested $80\%$ predictive band. A one-feature line on the setting reaches a held-out mean
absolute percentage error of $13.67\%$ on throughput and $12.78\%$ on power; the calibrated engine reaches $12.09\%$ on
throughput and $25.94\%$ on power, because its Bond draw is nearly flat ($196$ to $237$~kW) against measured values of
$187$ to $355$~kW. CrusherBayes, the Bayesian calibration with a Kennedy and O'Hagan structural discrepancy that the
product proposes, reaches $12.00\%$ and $14.31\%$ with $80\%$ intervals of coverage $0.80$ and $0.90$ that are $7\%$ and
$44\%$ narrower than the band of the calibrated engine; it does not beat the line on power. The surveys identify the
capacity base (posterior over prior standard deviation $0.45$), the noise and the discrepancy slopes on the setting, and
leave the classification and breakage scalars at their prior. A learned surrogate emulates the engine at $R^2$ from
$0.979$ to $0.998$ on an independent draw of $2196$ points.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords.} Comminution, cone crusher, population balance, Whiten model, Bond, Morrell, Bayesian
calibration, structural discrepancy, leave-one-out validation, neural surrogate, mineral processing.
