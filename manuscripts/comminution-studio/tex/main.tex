\begin{abstract}
A cone crusher turns a feed into a product whose size, throughput and power are set by four levers: the closed-side
setting, the eccentric throw, the eccentric speed and the feed size distribution. ChancaDEM is an open studio that
couples a Whiten population balance, an Evertsson capacity model and Bond's power law in one engine, runs it in the
browser, and holds it against ten measured surveys of a Metso HP500 secondary cone crusher at an itabirite iron-ore
operation (Rocha et al., 2024, CC BY). This report states the problem, the data, every method and the evaluation
protocol, and reports one benchmark on the same ten surveys: the calibrated engine, a bounded residual correction, a
free-form fit, a setting-only line, Morrell's size-specific energy and CrusherBayes, a Bayesian calibration of the engine
with a Kennedy and O'Hagan structural discrepancy sampled by differential-evolution moves. Every method is refit
without the held-out survey (strict leave-one-survey-out), with a label-shuffle control and a nested $80\%$
predictive band. The setting-only line, with one feature, reaches a held-out mean absolute percentage error of
$13.67\%$ on throughput and $12.78\%$ on power; CrusherBayes reaches $12.00\%$ and $14.31\%$, with $80\%$ bands
of coverage $0.80$ and $0.90$ that are $7\%$ and $44\%$ narrower than the band of the calibrated engine.
CrusherBayes does not beat the line on power. The ten surveys identify the capacity scale, the noise and the
discrepancy slopes on the setting, and leave the classification, breakage and $t_{10}$ scalars at their prior.
Morrell's energy, with its crushing work index and no-load power fitted per fold because the ore's index is not
published, reaches $38.08\%$ and, with a no-load term whose fitted index is negative in every fold, $15.21\%$.
The discrete-element package that the product plans is not part of the results.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem and Executive Context}\label{sec:problem}

\IEEEPARstart{C}{rushing} is the first stage of comminution, and its setting decides how much work the grinding mill
downstream must do. In Chilean copper mining the concentrator took $56{,}970$~TJ, $55.7\%$ of the $102{,}307$~TJ of
electricity the sector used in 2024, and the electricity per tonne of fine copper in concentrates rose from $8{,}946$ to
$13{,}079$~MJ between 2010 and 2024, an increase of $46.2\%$, while the national average ore grade fell from $0.75\%$
to $0.62\%$~\cite{cochilco2025}. The same agency attributes the rise to deeper deposits and harder rock that
``directly affect comminution'' and projects the electricity of the copper sector to grow by $20.2\%$ from 2025 to 2034,
from $27.6$ to $33.2$~TWh, against $8.3\%$ for fine copper~\cite{cochilco2026}. The decision that follows belongs to
the process engineer and the plant operator: at which setting, speed and feed condition to run a crusher, and how much
of the size reduction to leave to the mill.

Three outcomes are measured on a plant: the product size ($P_{80}$, the size passing $80\%$ of the mass), the throughput
and the motor power. They depend on the four levers through a non-linear chain: a tighter setting gives a finer product
and less capacity, the capacity rises with eccentric speed up to an optimum and falls past it, and a wider throw
coarsens the product at a fixed setting. Mechanistic models of this chain exist and are calibrated on survey data of
the machine; the practical question is how well a calibrated model predicts a survey it was not fitted to, because
the calibrations in the literature are judged on the surveys they were fitted on, and ten surveys leave little room
for a free-form correction. The report measures that, with a one-feature baseline and an uncertainty band that a
decision maker can read.

\section{Data}\label{sec:data}

\subsection{The ten HP500 surveys}

The measured data are Table A1 of Rocha et al.~\cite{rocha2024}: ten surveys of a Metso HP500 secondary cone crusher
(CC BY 4.0), each with the closed-side setting (CSS), the feed $F_{80}$, the feed rate and the motor power. Across the
ten surveys the CSS spans $38$ to $55$~mm (mean $46.9$), the feed $F_{80}$ $47$ to $140$~mm (mean $87.3$), the feed
rate $813$ to $1639$~t/h (mean $1145.9$) and the power $187$ to $355$~kW (mean $288.0$). The power is correlated with
the setting ($r=-0.82$) and with the feed rate ($r=-0.85$), and the feed rate with the setting ($r=0.81$): a tighter
setting draws more power at a lower feed rate. The product size distributions of the paper are figures, not tables, so
$P_{80}$ is not measured here and is never a target; the engine's $P_{80}$ at a survey is a model output. The throw
(34~mm) and speed (360~rpm) are the nominal values of the machine.

\subsection{The cases}

The studio carries 27 cases: 17 circuit cases that span the machine classes (two gyratory, two jaw, six secondary cone,
two tertiary cone, two short-head cone, and three controls: a pass-through case, an invalid case and a synthetic anchor)
and the ten surveys. The circuit cases are illustrative of their machine class and are not calibrated; only the HP500
lane is calibrated, to one machine on one ore. Every prediction passes a unit check in the bake: it must be finite and
lie inside the observable's range widened by a full range on each side, so that a throughput is never read as a power.

\section{Related Work and Methods}\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Population balance, capacity and power}

The product gradation follows the Whiten classification and breakage balance~\cite{whiten}. For a feed vector
$\mathbf f$, a classification matrix $\mathbf C$ and a breakage matrix $\mathbf B$,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf p = (\mathbf I-\mathbf C)\,(\mathbf I-\mathbf B\mathbf C)^{-1}\,\mathbf f .
\end{equation}
The classification uses the Andersen and Napier-Munn form with the constants $K_1$, $K_2$ and $K_3$, where $K_2$
tracks the open-side setting~\cite{napier}. The breakage matrix comes from the JKMRC $t_{10}$ energy-fineness relation
and an appearance function~\cite{napier}. The capacity follows the flow model of Evertsson~\cite{evertsson99,evertsson},
unimodal in eccentric speed. The power follows Bond's third theory~\cite{bond},
$W = 10\,W_i\,(1/\sqrt{P_{80}} - 1/\sqrt{F_{80}})$ in kWh/t, multiplied by the throughput. The feed is a
Rosin--Rammler distribution on a $\sqrt2$ sieve grid. The engine exists as a canonical Python implementation and a
TypeScript port that runs in the browser; the two agree to a relative difference of $2.3\times10^{-12}$ on a shared
reference of 121 operating points, and one evaluation takes about $1.3$~ms.

\subsection{The calibration of Rocha et al. as replicated}

Rocha et al.~\cite{rocha2024} fit an Andersen--Whiten model to the ten surveys: $K_1 = 0.23\,\mathrm{CSS}+0.30\,f_{80}$,
$K_2 = 12.0+0.55\,\mathrm{CSS}+0.40\,f_{80}$, $K_3=2.3$, $t_{10}=64-0.12\,\mathrm{CSS}-0.23\,f_{80}$, a capacity base
$Q_T\approx647$~t/h and a power form $P_c=\zeta P_d+P_n$ with $\zeta=1.30$ and $P_n=110$~kW. The engine carries these
constants (the calibrated backbone) and is run at each survey's measured setting and $f_{80}$. The paper's power
constants apply to its size-specific net power $P_d$, not to the Bond draw of the engine; applied to the Bond draw they
give a mean absolute percentage error of $47.58\%$ in sample, and the bare Bond draw $26.88\%$ (Table~\ref{tab:parity}).
The Bond draw is nearly flat ($196$ to $237$~kW) against a measured spread of $187$ to $355$~kW. A least-squares refit
of the paper's form on the Bond draw reaches $19.28\%$ in sample with a negative slope $\zeta=-2.41$ and
$P_n=829$~kW, which is a fit to the survey mean and not a physical constant.

\subsection{Setting-only line}

The baseline for every method is an ordinary-least-squares line of one target on the closed-side setting alone,
$y=a+b\,\mathrm{CSS}$, for throughput and for power, refit on the training surveys of each fold. It carries no physics.
Its value is that the claim of any other method is the margin over it.

\subsection{Bounded residual and free-form fits}

Two corrections to the calibrated backbone are held out beside it. The bounded residual adds a ridge regression
($\lambda=1$) on the standardized setting and feed $f_{80}$, clipped to $\pm20\%$ of the backbone value. The free-form
fit is an unconstrained least-squares regression of the raw target on the same two features. A constant-mean
predictor and a label-shuffle control (the residual targets permuted) bound the skill from below.

\subsection{Morrell's size-specific energy}

Morrell's method gives the specific energy of a conventional crusher from the feed and product sizes and the crushing
work index $M_{ic}$~\cite{morrell09,gmsg}. With the equations B4 and B5 of the guideline~\cite{gmsg},
\begin{align}
W_c &= S_c\,K_2\,M_{ic}\;4\left(x_2^{f(x_2)} - x_1^{f(x_1)}\right)\ [\mathrm{kWh/t}],\\
f(x) &= -\left(0.295 + x/10^{6}\right),\qquad S_c = K_s\,(x_1x_2)^{-0.2},
\end{align}
with $x_1$ and $x_2$ the feed and product $P_{80}$ in micrometres, $K_s=55$, $K_2=1.0$ for a crusher in closed circuit
with a classifying screen, and $S_c$ applied when it is below one and taken as one above. The power is $W_c$ times the
throughput. The index $M_{ic}$ of the Minas Rio itabirite is not published, so it is a fitted scalar, and so is the
no-load power; both are refit on the nine training surveys of every fold. The design column of a survey uses the
measured $f_{80}$, the measured feed rate and the engine's $P_{80}$ (a model output used as an input). Two variants
are held out: $\mathrm{kW}=M_{ic}\,g$, and $\mathrm{kW}=M_{ic}\,g+P_{noload}$. The primary paper was not opened; the
equations are taken from the guideline.

\subsection{Learned surrogate and denoising autoencoder}

A multilayer perceptron (11-64-64-32-10) trained on a Latin-hypercube sweep of the engine ($6430$ training points)
emulates its ten outputs, so that what-if exploration is instant; the approach follows the surrogate of a
phenomenological comminution model of Koh et al.~\cite{koh}. A denoising autoencoder (14-16-6-16-14) scores how far a
point lies from the training manifold, as a warning that the surrogate is asked to extrapolate. Both are scored on an
independent second draw ($2196$ points, seed $67890$), through onnxruntime on the graph the browser runs. Both emulate
the engine, not the plant, and neither is a predictor for the HP500 surveys.

\section{Proposed Method: CrusherBayes}\label{sec:cb}

Rocha et al.'s calibration is a point fit. CrusherBayes places a prior on the engine's fitted scalars, adds an explicit
structural discrepancy per target in the form of Kennedy and O'Hagan~\cite{kennedy}, and returns a posterior
predictive distribution for a survey that was not used in the fit. For survey $i$ and target $t$ (throughput in t/h,
power in kW),
\begin{equation}
y_{t,i} = f_t(\boldsymbol\theta;\,\mathrm{CSS}_i, f_{80,i}) + \delta_t(\mathbf z_i) + \varepsilon_{t,i},
\end{equation}
\begin{align}
\delta_t(\mathbf z) &= d_{t0} + d_{t1} z_{\mathrm{CSS}} + d_{t2} z_{f_{80}},\\
d_{tj}&\sim N(0,\tau_t^2),\qquad \varepsilon_{t,i}\sim N(0,\sigma_t^2),
\end{align}
with $\mathbf z$ the setting and feed $f_{80}$ standardized on the training surveys, $\tau=140$~t/h and $36$~kW, and
$\boldsymbol\theta$ the eight engine scalars of Table~\ref{tab:priors}. Given $\boldsymbol\theta$ the discrepancy and
the noise are Gaussian, so the discrepancy is integrated out exactly,
$\mathbf y_t\sim N\!\left(f_t(\boldsymbol\theta),\,\sigma_t^2\mathbf I+\tau_t^2\mathbf Z\mathbf Z^{\top}\right)$,
and the sampler explores ten dimensions (the eight scalars and the two noise scales); the discrepancy coefficients are
then drawn from their exact Gaussian conditional. The noise has $\ln\sigma_t\sim N(\ln 60\ \mathrm{t/h},1)$ and
$N(\ln 15\ \mathrm{kW},1)$. The engine is its own simulator, so no emulator is used.

The sampler is the ensemble sampler \texttt{emcee} $3.1.6$~\cite{emcee} with differential-evolution moves
(\texttt{DEMove} $0.8$ and \texttt{DESnookerMove} $0.2$)~\cite{terbraak06,terbraak08}: $20$ walkers, $7000$ steps,
$1000$ discarded, thinned by $10$, seeded. The affine-invariant stretch move of Goodman and Weare~\cite{goodman} was
tried first and did not converge: its autocorrelation time stayed above $100$ steps and grew with the chain, along the
parameters the surveys do not identify. A posterior is recorded as converged when the post-burn chain is at least
$50$ integrated autocorrelation times long. The predictive interval of a held-out survey is the 10th to 90th
percentile of $20$ predictive draws per retained posterior sample.

\section{Evaluation Protocol}\label{sec:protocol}

The protocol is shared by every method and is called CrusherCal.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Strict leave-one-survey-out.} For each of the ten surveys, every fitted quantity (the capacity base, the
power-form constants, the ridge and free-form coefficients, the line, $M_{ic}$ and the no-load power, and the whole
CrusherBayes posterior) is refit on the other nine, and the held-out survey enters only the prediction. A test plants an
absurd target in one survey and shows that every other fold moves and its own does not.
\item \textbf{Leaky folds.} The same fits on all ten surveys expose the optimism of an evaluation that lets the held-out
survey into the fit.
\item \textbf{Controls.} A constant-mean predictor, and a label-shuffle control that must not beat the backbone.
\item \textbf{Nested $80\%$ band.} The band of the calibrated backbone is the prediction $\pm1.2816\,\sigma$, with
$\sigma$ of each survey taken from the held-out residuals of the other nine surveys only. A band whose $\sigma$ comes
from the same ten residuals it is checked against is in sample, and is reported only to show the difference.
\item \textbf{Scores.} MAPE in percent, RMSE in the target's unit, $R^2$, and for the bands the share of surveys inside
the interval (coverage) and the mean width in the target's unit. With $n=10$, one survey moves a coverage by $0.1$, and a
difference below about $3$ MAPE points is within noise.
\item \textbf{Stopping criterion stated before the run.} CrusherBayes is dropped as a predictor if (a) its power MAPE is
not below that of the line by at least $3$ points and its throughput MAPE is not below that of the calibrated
backbone by at least $3$ points, and (b) on either target its coverage lies outside $0.7$ to $0.9$ or its mean band is
wider than the band of the calibrated backbone. The identifiability table is reported in either case.
\end{itemize}

Table~\ref{tab:bench} and Fig.~\ref{fig:loo} give the benchmark. On throughput the methods that use the setting and
the capacity physics sit between $12.00\%$ and $13.67\%$, inside the noise of $n=10$, and all beat the constant mean
($18.29\%$); the label-shuffle control ($25.04\%$) is worse than the backbone, as it must be. CrusherBayes has the
lowest throughput MAPE ($12.00\%$) and the narrowest band ($439$~t/h against $474$~t/h for the backbone, $7\%$
narrower), at coverage $0.80$.

On power the picture is different. The calibrated backbone, whose power is the Bond draw refit in the paper's form,
is worse than the constant mean ($25.94\%$ against $24.48\%$) because the Bond draw is nearly flat while the measured
power is not. The setting-only line is the best on power ($12.78\%$), followed by the free-form fit ($14.03\%$),
CrusherBayes ($14.31\%$) and Morrell with a no-load term ($15.21\%$). CrusherBayes is $1.53$ points behind the line,
which is within the noise of $n=10$ and is not a gain. Its power band ($118$~kW) is $44\%$ narrower than the band of
the calibrated backbone ($210$~kW) at coverage $0.90$ and equals the width of the line's band ($120$~kW), which has
coverage $0.80$. The stopping criterion of Section~\ref{sec:protocol} is evaluated as written: condition (a) holds
(power $-1.53$ points against the line, throughput $+0.09$ points against the backbone), and condition (b) does not
(coverage $0.80$ and $0.90$, both inside $0.7$ to $0.9$, and both bands narrower than the backbone's). The rule does not
drop the method. The result is a calibrated band, not a predictive gain over the line.

\subsection{Optimism of an evaluation that leaks}

Table~\ref{tab:leaky} gives the same methods when the held-out survey is allowed into the fit. The fits that adapt
to the data gain most: the bounded residual falls from $13.45\%$ to $8.51\%$ on throughput and from $25.37\%$ to
$12.00\%$ on power, and the free-form fit from $14.03\%$ to $9.46\%$ on power. The calibrated backbone, with one
scalar, gains less ($12.09\%$ to $10.95\%$ on throughput). The label-shuffle control on power falls from $40.19\%$ to
$15.50\%$, which shows how much of the apparent skill of a leaky evaluation is the fit absorbing the survey.

\subsection{Morrell's size-specific energy}

Without a no-load term the fit is one positive scalar, $M_{ic}$ between $3.70$ and $4.21$~kWh/t across the folds, and
the held-out error is $38.08\%$, the largest of the size-aware models. With a no-load term the error falls to
$15.21\%$, but the fit is not Morrell's physics: in every fold the fitted $M_{ic}$ is negative ($-6.29$ to
$-4.16$~kWh/t) with a no-load power of $591$ to $727$~kW, about twice the largest measured power. The measured power
falls as the throughput rises, and the design column rises with it, so the least-squares line has a negative slope.
The variant is a descriptive line on that column and not an estimate of $M_{ic}$. Both variants are worse than the
setting-only line; the difference of $2.4$ points for the no-load variant is within the noise, and the $25$ points of
the variant without it are not. The size-specific energy adds no predictive skill on these ten surveys. An SMC Test of
the ore and a no-load reading of the machine would settle the fit.

\subsection{What the ten surveys identify}

\begin{figure}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig-contraction.pdf}
\caption{Posterior over prior standard deviation of each CrusherBayes parameter, fit to all ten surveys. Below $0.5$
(dashed line) the surveys identify the parameter; near $1$ (dotted line) it stays at its prior.}
\label{fig:contraction}
\end{figure}

Fig.~\ref{fig:contraction} gives the contraction of the posterior against the prior. The capacity base $Q_T$
(contraction $0.45$, posterior mean $646$~t/h against the nominal $647$), the two noise scales
($0.30$ and $0.33$) and the discrepancy slopes on the setting and on $f_{80}$ ($0.34$ to $0.38$) are identified. The
posterior noise is $145$~t/h and $38.8$~kW, much larger than the prior medians ($60$~t/h and $15$~kW), and the
discrepancy slope on the setting is large ($+100.8$~t/h and $-57.1$~kW per standardized unit): the engine's dependence
on the setting differs from the measured one, in power by about $57$~kW per standard deviation of the setting. The work
index, $\zeta$ and $P_n$ contract weakly ($0.85$, $0.73$ and $0.80$; $\zeta$ and $W_i$ enter the power only as a
product). The $K_1$ and $K_2$ multipliers, $K_3$, the $t_{10}$ shift and the discrepancy constants stay at their prior
(contraction $0.88$ to $1.03$). The scalars that set the product size are not identified because the two measured
targets carry the product size only through the Bond term of the power, and the power follows the setting, not the
product size. Ten surveys without a measured product size cannot calibrate the population balance.

All eleven posteriors (ten folds and the fit to all surveys) are converged under the criterion of $50$ autocorrelation
times: the largest autocorrelation time is $48$ to $85$ steps and the $6000$ post-burn steps are $71$ to $125$ times
it, with acceptance fractions between $0.17$ and $0.20$.

\section{Limits and Negative Results}\label{sec:limits}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Sample size.} $n=10$ surveys, one machine, one ore. A difference below about $3$ MAPE points is noise,
and one survey moves a coverage by $0.1$. The coverage of $0.80$ and $0.90$ are coarse estimates.
\item \textbf{CrusherBayes does not beat the setting-only line on power} ($14.31\%$ against $12.78\%$), and beats the
calibrated backbone on throughput by $0.09$ points. Its contribution on these data is a calibrated and narrower band
and the identifiability result.
\item \textbf{The population-balance scalars are not identified.} $K_1$, $K_2$, $K_3$ and the $t_{10}$ shift stay at
their prior, because $P_{80}$ is not measured. Their posterior is the prior and must not be read as an estimate.
\item \textbf{The priors' widths are the author's.} No published range of these fitted scalars for this ore was found.
\item \textbf{Bond on the measured power.} The Bond draw does not track the measured power spread; the paper's power
constants applied to it give $47.58\%$ in sample.
\item \textbf{Morrell.} $M_{ic}$ and the no-load power are fitted, not measured; the negative fitted $M_{ic}$ of the
no-load variant means that variant is descriptive. The equations are taken from the guideline; the primary paper was
not opened. Only the conventional-crusher branch of the method is used.
\item \textbf{Scope of the engine.} The studio couples calibrated models, not a plant. The synthetic machines are not
calibrated. The three-dimensional chamber view is a kinematic animation and not a discrete-element solve. The
learned models emulate the engine, not a plant.
\item \textbf{Discrete-element simulation.} The package that is planned for it is not validated and no result of it is
reported.
\item \textbf{Prior art.} Searches found no Bayesian calibration with structural discrepancy of a cone-crusher
population-balance model evaluated held out; they were not exhaustive (Scopus and Web of Science were not searched).
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}

On ten industrial surveys of one cone crusher, a one-feature line on the closed-side setting is a hard baseline: it
reaches $13.67\%$ on throughput and $12.78\%$ on power held out, and the calibrated physics, a free-form fit, Morrell's
size-specific energy and a Bayesian calibration with structural discrepancy all land within the noise of it or worse.
CrusherBayes gives a posterior predictive band of the right coverage that is $44\%$ narrower than the band of the
calibrated engine on power and $7\%$ narrower on throughput, shows that ten surveys identify the capacity scale, the
noise and the setting dependence of the discrepancy, and shows that they cannot identify the classification and
breakage scalars. Measuring the product size distribution of each survey is the data that would change that.
