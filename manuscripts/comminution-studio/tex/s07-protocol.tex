\section{Experimental protocol}\label{sec:protocol}

\subsection{Cases and what each set of cases tests}\label{sec:protocol-cases}

Three sets of operating points serve three different questions, and no result of one set is read as evidence about another. The
seventeen circuit cases test the behaviour of the coupled engine: whether it reproduces the size-reduction ladder of a crushing circuit
and the trends of each lever, and whether its checks flag the controls. They are baked at their operating points and judged against the
numeric expected ranges each case declares (for example, for S01, a $P_{80}$ between $22$ and $35$~mm, a throughput between $520$ and
$780$~t/h and a reduction ratio between $3.5$ and $5.5$), ranges written from engineering relations and not from the engine's own value.
The ten HP500 surveys test prediction against measurement, for the two measured targets only, under the leave-one-survey-out protocol of
Section~\ref{sec:m-crushercal}. The two Latin-hypercube draws test the learned models against the engine. Each case also has two
variants computed live in the browser, a tight setting ($15\%$ lower) and an open setting ($15\%$ higher), which the studio shows and which
are not scored.

\subsection{Metrics}\label{sec:protocol-metrics}

For a target with measured values $y_i$ and held-out predictions $\hat y_i$, $i=1,\dots,N_s$, the point scores are
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{MAPE} = \frac{100}{N_s}\sum_{i=1}^{N_s}\left|\frac{\hat y_i - y_i}{y_i}\right|,\qquad
\mathrm{RMSE} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N_s}\sum_{i=1}^{N_s}\left(\hat y_i - y_i\right)^2},\qquad
R^2 = 1 - \frac{\sum_i(y_i-\hat y_i)^2}{\sum_i(y_i-\bar y)^2},
\label{eq:scores}
\end{equation}
and the largest deviation $\max_i|\hat y_i-y_i|/y_i$ in percent. A held-out $R^2$ can be negative: a predictor worse than the mean of the
measured values scores below zero. For an interval $[\ell_i,u_i]$ the coverage and the mean width are
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{cov} = \frac{1}{N_s}\sum_{i=1}^{N_s}\mathbb 1\!\left[\ell_i\le y_i\le u_i\right],\qquad
\bar w = \frac{1}{N_s}\sum_{i=1}^{N_s}(u_i-\ell_i),
\label{eq:coverage}
\end{equation}
and the mean log predictive density of a predictive distribution with density $p_i$ is $\overline{\mathrm{lpd}}=\frac{1}{N_s}\sum_i\log p_i(y_i)$.
For CrusherBayes $p_i$ is the posterior predictive density, estimated as the log of the mean over the retained samples of the Gaussian
predictive density at each sample; for a residual-band predictor it is the normal density with standard deviation $\sigma_{-i}$ at its
residual. The contraction of a parameter is the ratio of its posterior to its prior standard deviation on its natural scale; below $0.5$ the
surveys identify the parameter, and near one it stays at its prior. For the learned models, $R^2$ and MAPE are computed per output in the
original units over the $2196$ held-out points; the false-alarm rate is the share of held-out points that raise an alarm and the detection
rate the share of planted points that do.

\emph{Uncertainty of a held-out error.} Ten surveys give each MAPE a sampling error that is of the order of the differences between the
predictors, so every held-out MAPE of the surveys carries a nonparametric bootstrap interval over the surveys. With $a_{k,i}$ the absolute
percentage error of predictor $k$ at survey $i$ and $I^{(b)}$ the $b$-th of $B=\uqB$ resamples of the ten survey indices with replacement
(seed $\uqSeed$),
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{MAPE}^{(b)}_k = \frac{1}{N_s}\sum_{i\in I^{(b)}} a_{k,i},\qquad
\Delta^{(b)}_{k,l} = \mathrm{MAPE}^{(b)}_k - \mathrm{MAPE}^{(b)}_l ,
\label{eq:bootstrap}
\end{equation}
and the $95\%$ interval of a MAPE, or of the difference of two predictors, is the $2.5$th to the $97.5$th percentile of its $B$ values. The
same resampled surveys serve every predictor, so a difference is paired: a survey that is hard for both predictors moves both errors
together. A ranking of two predictors is stated only when the interval of their difference excludes zero; otherwise the two are reported as
not distinguishable on these surveys. The rule applies to every two predictors, not only to a predictor and the line or the backbone, so the
interval of every pair is computed on the same resamples (Table~\ref{tab:pairs}). The predictions are those of the committed documents (the strict CrusherCal predictions and the
CrusherBayes predictive means), except two that no document stores per survey: the constant mean of each fold, computed from the
measured values of its training surveys, and the label-shuffle control, recomputed with the product's own functions and seed on the
calibrated backbone of each fold at full precision. The computation is the report's script
\texttt{figures/\allowbreak{}heldout\_uq.py}, which recomputes every committed MAPE from these predictions before it resamples.

\subsection{Baselines and controls}\label{sec:protocol-baselines}

Every comparison has a baseline that a method must beat before it claims skill. On the surveys: the constant mean of the training surveys
(no information), the label-shuffle control (a fit that learns noise, which must not beat the backbone), and the setting-only line (one
feature, no physics), which is the bar for any claim on power. For the surrogate the baseline is the engine itself, which is what the slot
answers with when its bundle is invalid; for the autoencoder the baseline is the envelope check. The leaky variant of every survey fit is
reported beside the strict one, so that the optimism of letting a survey into its own fit is measured and not assumed. The published
calibration enters twice and is run, never quoted: its classification, $t_{10}$ and power constants are run in the engine, with the engine's
scalars refit per fold, and its separate throughput model (Equation~\eqref{eq:rocha}) is run on the ten surveys as a throughput predictor,
with its fitted factor as published and refit per fold. A predictor whose difference to the setting-only line has an interval that includes
zero has no claim of skill over the line on that target.

\subsection{The protocol shared by every survey predictor}\label{sec:protocol-shared}

All predictors of the surveys use the same ten folds, the same targets in the same units, the same score functions and the same nested band
construction (Section~\ref{sec:m-crushercal}); CrusherBayes uses its posterior predictive interval in place of the band. In every fold every
fitted quantity, the capacity base, the power-form constants, the ridge and free-form coefficients, the line, $M_{ic}$ and the no-load power,
and the whole CrusherBayes posterior, is refit on the nine training surveys, and the held-out survey enters only the prediction. A test plants
an absurd target in one survey and checks that every other fold's prediction moves and the planted survey's own does not. With $N_s=10$ the
protocol has a resolution of $0.1$ in coverage, and the uncertainty of the errors is the bootstrap of Section~\ref{sec:protocol-metrics}. The
adoption rule of CrusherBayes uses a margin of three points of MAPE, fixed before the run as a rule; it is not a measured noise level, and the
bootstrap intervals of the differences between the predictors are wider (Section~\ref{sec:results-uq}). No predictor is tuned on the held-out scores: every constant of every fit was set before the first run and is
recorded in its document.

\subsection{Seeds, repetitions, hardware and run times}\label{sec:protocol-seeds}

The survey evaluation is deterministic given the seed $42$, which fixes the label-shuffle permutation (one per target, shared by the strict
and the leaky folds); CrusherBayes spawns one seed per
posterior from a seed sequence started at $42$, uses that seed plus one for the predictive draws of the posterior, and its document is byte-identical whatever the number of worker
processes. The learned models use the training seed $12345$ and the held-out seed $67890$, with torch single-threaded. The
particle-replacement check of Section~\ref{sec:validation} uses five seeds per parcel count. The laboratory-jaw runs of Section~\ref{sec:protocol-dem} use the seed $\demSeed$. Each posterior is one chain of twenty walkers;
its repetition is the eleven posteriors of the folds, whose convergence diagnostics are reported. The bootstrap of the held-out errors uses the seed $\uqSeed$. The measured run times, recorded on \resDate{} on a
Windows 11 workstation with Python $\resPython$ and Node $24$, are in Table~\ref{tab:resources}: about $\resBakeMin$~minutes for the whole bake,
of which about $\resHpMin$ are the HP500 step with the CrusherBayes sampler ($140{,}000$ log-probability evaluations per posterior, eleven
posteriors), $\resModelsS$~s to train and evaluate both learned models on the processor, and a median of $\resEngineMed$~ms per engine
evaluation. No stage uses a graphics processor.

\subsection{The laboratory jaw: three sides at three operating points}\label{sec:protocol-dem}

The discrete element method of Section~\ref{sec:m-dem} and the laboratory-jaw evaluation of the population balance of Section~\ref{sec:m-labjaw} are compared with a measurement under one protocol, whose question differs from that of the surveys: the surveys ask which predictor of a plant crusher generalises to a survey it was not fitted on, while the laboratory jaw asks whether each of two models, no input of whose code reads the jaw's measurements, reproduces three measured quantities, and how far they are from each other. Nothing is held out and nothing is fitted.

\emph{The operating points and why these.} The operating points are the three narrow feed classes of the laboratory tests, $63/52$, $31.5/26.5$ and $16/13.2$~mm, at the stroke frequency of $6.0$~Hz and the closed-side setting of $7.5$~mm, with batches of $6$~kg. They are the only points of the paper at which a throughput, a net power and a product size distribution were all measured (Section~\ref{sec:data-lab}): the frequencies and settings of its Table 5 other than this one are simulated only, so a comparison there would be a comparison with nothing. The three points span the ratio of the feed to the open side ($17.5$~mm) from a feed whose spheres are three to four times larger than the open side to a feed that is smaller than it.

\emph{The three sides and the quantities.} Each point has three sides: the measurement (the paper's, digitised), the particle simulation (the package \texttt{crushdem} on the ore it derives from the paper's single-particle tests) and the population balance (the laboratory-jaw module with the first-estimate preset and the ore's $A\times b$ from Equation~\eqref{eq:labjaw-axb}). Three quantities are compared: the throughput $Q$ (kg/h), the net power $\bar P$ (kW; for the particle simulation, the mean power of the swing plate in the steady window of Equation~\eqref{eq:dem-window}) and the reduction ratio
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{RR}=\frac{F_{80}}{P_{80}},
\label{eq:proto-rr}
\end{equation}
with $F_{80}$ the analytic value of the feed class (Equation~\eqref{eq:labjaw-feed}), the same for the three sides, and $P_{80}$ read from each side's cumulative product curve. The reduction ratio is used in place of $P_{80}$ because the feed differs between the three points and a ratio against the analytic $F_{80}$ makes the three comparable. For a pair of sides, the first $X$ against the second $X_{\mathrm m}$ (the measurement, or the particle simulation when the population balance is set against it), the relative error and the decision are
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon=\frac{X-X_{\mathrm m}}{X_{\mathrm m}},\qquad
\text{decision}=\begin{cases}\text{met}, & \lvert\varepsilon\rvert\le b_{\mathrm{band}},\\ \text{not met}, & \lvert\varepsilon\rvert>b_{\mathrm{band}},\\ \text{not compared}, & X\ \text{or}\ X_{\mathrm m}\ \text{absent},\end{cases}
\label{eq:proto-band}
\end{equation}
so three pairs of sides and three quantities give nine decisions per operating point (Section~\ref{sec:data-dem}). The population balance predicts no power, so its two power decisions are always not compared. The specific energy that the measured power over the measured throughput gives, in J/kg, and the product size distribution, are reported with the deviation of each side and carry no band.

\emph{The bands.} The bands are $b_{\mathrm{band}}=0.10$ for the throughput, $0.30$ for the net power and $0.15$ for the reduction ratio. They were fixed in the design of the method, before any run of the comparison, from the best agreements that the literature reports between a discrete element simulation of a laboratory jaw and its measurements: a mean net power of $0.32$~kW simulated against $0.34$~kW measured for the $63/52$~mm feed in the paper itself~\cite{barrios2020}, and, for a quarry rock with polyhedral particles, a mean relative absolute deviation of $6.6$ percent on the throughput and $27$ percent on the power for a feed coarser than $37$~mm at a closed side of $25$~mm~\cite{tino2026}. No numerical tolerance between a crusher population balance and a discrete element simulation was found in the literature searched, so the bands on the population balance side are the same ones, not a published value, and the band of the reduction ratio has no published precedent (the agreement of the product size at the $25$~mm setting is called very good in that study without a number). The bands are set once for all three points and are not tuned: the document records when they were set.

\emph{The decision rule and its bases.} A decision is one of the three values of Equation~\eqref{eq:proto-band}. A decision that is not compared carries its basis: the particle run did not run the whole batch through (so no decision rests on it); the run gives no steady window; the run discharged nothing; or, for every power decision of the population balance, no work index of the ore is published, so that side states no power. A decision that rests on a window also states whether it is resolved by Equation~\eqref{eq:dem-resolved}: the nearer band edge more than two standard errors from the window mean. A decision that is met or not met is a statement about the comparison at the stated band, not about the model's validity: a model can meet a band by cancellation, and the specific energy and the product are reported so that the reader can see whether it does.

\emph{A run that does not complete.} A run ends in one of five states (complete, overlap error after the ladder, stalled, stopped at the stroke limit or at the wall-time limit; Section~\ref{sec:m-dem}). Only a complete run has a window and statistics; the window needs at least six strokes and three between its two marks. A run that does not complete is recorded as the package leaves it, with the stroke at which it stopped, the mass it had passed and the spheres it held, and no decision that needs a window is stated for it; it is never repeated with bounds relaxed beyond the package's own ladder, so that one protocol holds on both sides, and the state of the document is \emph{partial}. A run that completes only at the second rung of the ladder is a complete run at a relaxed overlap limit, which the document records with the strokes of its window above the reference overlap.

\emph{Seed and repetition.} Each point is run once, with one fixed seed (the same for the three points and recorded in the document). The standard error of Equation~\eqref{eq:dem-stats} is the scatter of the strokes of one window, with its autocorrelation, and it does not include the spread between seeds: no repetition with another seed is made, so the intervals describe one realisation of the model and a resolved decision does not say that another seed would agree. The measurements carry their digitisation noise (Table~\ref{tab:lab-measured}) and no experimental scatter, because the paper reports none; the population balance carries its sensitivity band and no probability. No input of the product's code reads a measured throughput, power or product of the jaw, and no ore property was tuned to reach a band; two choices of the ore's derivation were made by the package's authors after jaw results of the package were known (Section~\ref{sec:m-dem}), and the sensitivity of the met net-power decisions to them was not run. When the measured values are replaced by others, the particle simulation and the population balance are unchanged and only the decisions move.

\emph{Acceptance criteria.} The two methods are judged by criteria of correctness and of reporting, as the other methods are (Table~\ref{tab:acceptance}): the discrete element method by the package's tests of its integrator, contact law, walls, neighbour search and breakage (Section~\ref{sec:m-dem}) and by the product's checks on the document; the population balance of the laboratory jaw by its reproduction of the paper's Table 2 and the tests of its module (Section~\ref{sec:m-labjaw}). The package's own validation protocol also sets the target that the particle simulation reproduce the mean net power within $30$ percent and the throughput within $10$ percent, which the package reports as met or not met and does not use to fail its build: a method that misses a band passes its acceptance criterion when the miss is computed as stated and reported as it is.
