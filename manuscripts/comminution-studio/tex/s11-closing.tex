\section{Reproducibility}\label{sec:repro}

\emph{Repository and release.} The source code, the data, the committed artifacts and the sources of this report are in the public repository
\url{https://github.com/fsantibanezleal/CAOS_ChancaDEM} (MIT licence for the code and the product's own data; CC BY 4.0 for the survey table and
everything derived from it). Every number of the report is read from the artifacts of release 0.12.001; the studio runs at
\url{https://chancadem.fasl-work.com}.

\emph{Commands.} From the repository root, with the pipeline environment of \texttt{requirements-precompute.txt}:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{python data-pipeline/\allowbreak{}run.py all} bakes the $27$ cases, the three HP500 documents (the sampler of CrusherBayes runs in this step), the index
and the readiness document; \texttt{python data-pipeline/\allowbreak{}run.py hp500} rebuilds only the three HP500 documents;
\item \texttt{python data-pipeline/\allowbreak{}run.py models} draws the sweeps, trains and exports the two learned models and scores them held out (torch on the
processor);
\item \texttt{python manuscripts/\allowbreak{}comminution-studio/\allowbreak{}figures/\allowbreak{}make\_tables.py} regenerates every table of the report from the committed artifacts, the
catalog and the schemas, recomputes the engine parity figure, and writes the digests of \texttt{data/\allowbreak{}sha256.txt};
\item \texttt{python manuscripts/\allowbreak{}comminution-studio/\allowbreak{}figures/\allowbreak{}make\_figs.py} regenerates the thirteen figures as vector PDF (matplotlib, from the
notebook environment); two of them call the engine code (the chamber functions and the particle-replacement walk);
\item \texttt{latexmk -pdf main.tex}, run in \texttt{manuscripts/\allowbreak{}comminution-studio/\allowbreak{}tex}, builds the report.
\end{itemize}

\emph{Versions.} Python $3.13$ on the workstation ($3.12$ in continuous integration), numpy $2.4.6$, emcee $3.1.6$, torch $2.12.1$ on the processor, onnx
$1.22.0$, onnxruntime $1.27.0$ and onnxruntime-web $1.27.0$, matplotlib $3.11.2$, Node $24$. The committed artifacts record the engine release 0.12.001
and the seeds $42$ (surveys), $12345$ and $67890$ (learned models).

\emph{Archived artifacts.} Table~\ref{tab:hashes} gives the SHA-256 digests of the artifacts the report reads; the full digests are in
\texttt{manuscripts/\allowbreak{}comminution-studio/\allowbreak{}data/\allowbreak{}sha256.txt}, and the CrusherBayes document records the digest of its own inputs
(\texttt{483d24c5\ldots b0ce605a}).

\emph{Notebooks.} \texttt{notebooks/\allowbreak{}en} and \texttt{notebooks/\allowbreak{}es} hold the same didactic notebooks, from getting started and the size distributions and the breakage and
classification functions to the capacity and the power, the real surveys, the held-out validation, the learned models, Morrell's energy and CrusherBayes,
on the product's own data and modules.

\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}

On ten industrial surveys of one cone crusher, a calibrated Whiten, Evertsson and Bond engine predicts the throughput of a survey it has not seen with a
mean absolute percentage error of $12.09\%$, against $11.55\%$ in sample and $18.29\%$ for the constant mean, with a nested $80\%$ band that covers eight
surveys of ten. It does not predict the power: its Bond draw is flat across the surveys, and its held-out power error of $25.94\%$ is worse than the constant
mean. A line on the closed-side setting alone is the hardest baseline on power ($12.78\%$), and neither Morrell's size-specific energy with a fitted work
index ($38.08\%$, and $15.21\%$ with a no-load term whose fitted index is negative in every fold) nor CrusherBayes ($14.31\%$) passes it. CrusherBayes gives
held-out intervals of the stated coverage that are $44\%$ narrower than the backbone band on power and $7\%$ on throughput, and its posterior shows that the
ten surveys identify the capacity base, the noise and the dependence of the discrepancy on the setting, and cannot identify the classification and breakage
scalars. A leaky evaluation would have ranked the bounded residual first; held out it is behind the backbone. The next steps are measurements, not models: the
product size distribution of each survey, which the population balance needs to be calibrated; the crushing work index and the no-load power of the machine,
which would turn Morrell's method into a prediction; and more surveys, which would resolve differences of a few points that ten cannot.
