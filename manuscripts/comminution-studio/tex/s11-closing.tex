\section{Reproducibility}\label{sec:repro}

\emph{Repository and release.} The source code, the data, the committed artifacts and the sources of this report are in the public repository
\url{https://github.com/fsantibanezleal/CAOS_ChancaDEM} (MIT licence for the code and the product's own data; CC BY 4.0 for the survey table and
everything derived from it). Every number of the report is read from the artifacts of release \docrelease, or computed from them by the report's scripts; the studio runs at
\url{https://chancadem.fasl-work.com}.

\emph{Commands.} From the repository root, with the pipeline environment of \texttt{requirements-precompute.txt}:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{python data-pipeline/\allowbreak{}run.py all} bakes the $27$ cases, the four HP500 documents (the sampler of CrusherBayes runs in this step), the index
and the readiness document; \texttt{python data-pipeline/\allowbreak{}run.py hp500} rebuilds only the four HP500 documents, and
\texttt{python data-pipeline/\allowbreak{}run.py comparison} only the paired comparison, from the two documents it reads;
\item \texttt{python data-pipeline/\allowbreak{}run.py models} draws the sweeps, trains and exports the two learned models and scores them held out (torch on the
processor);
\item \texttt{python manuscripts/\allowbreak{}comminution-studio/\allowbreak{}figures/\allowbreak{}make\_tables.py} regenerates every table of the report from the committed artifacts, the
catalog and the schemas, recomputes the engine parity figure, runs the bootstrap over the surveys and the published throughput model
(\texttt{heldout\_uq.py}), and writes the digests of \texttt{data/\allowbreak{}sha256.txt};
\item \texttt{python manuscripts/\allowbreak{}comminution-studio/\allowbreak{}figures/\allowbreak{}measure\_resources.py} records the measured resources of the stages, each run into a sandbox
output root under a process-tree monitor, and the engine time per case, in \texttt{data/\allowbreak{}resources.json};
\item \texttt{python manuscripts/\allowbreak{}comminution-studio/\allowbreak{}figures/\allowbreak{}make\_figs.py} regenerates the fourteen figures as vector PDF (matplotlib, from the
notebook environment); three of them call the engine code (the chamber functions, the drift response of survey 1 and the
particle-replacement walk);
\item \texttt{latexmk -pdf main.tex}, run in \texttt{manuscripts/\allowbreak{}comminution-studio/\allowbreak{}tex}, builds the report.
\end{itemize}

\emph{Versions.} Python $\resPython$ in the pipeline and notebook environments of the workstation (Python $3.12$ in continuous integration), numpy $2.4.6$,
emcee $3.1.6$, torch $2.12.1$ on the processor, onnx $1.22.0$, onnxruntime $1.27.0$ and onnxruntime-web $1.27.0$, matplotlib $3.11.2$, Node $24$. The
committed artifacts record the release of the engine that baked them in their \texttt{engine\_version} field, which the readiness document compares with
the product version to mark a document stale, and the seeds $42$ (surveys), $12345$ and $67890$ (learned models).

\emph{Archived artifacts.} Table~\ref{tab:hashes} gives the SHA-256 digests of the artifacts the report reads; the full digests are in
\texttt{manuscripts/\allowbreak{}comminution-studio/\allowbreak{}data/\allowbreak{}sha256.txt}, and the CrusherBayes document records the digest of its own inputs

\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}

On ten industrial surveys of one cone crusher, a calibrated Whiten, Evertsson and Bond engine predicts the throughput of a survey it has not seen with a
mean absolute percentage error of $12.09\%$ ($95\%$ interval $\uqQBblo$ to $\uqQBbhi$), against $11.55\%$ in sample and $18.29\%$ for the constant
mean, with a nested $80\%$ band that covers eight surveys of ten; the published throughput model of Rocha et al., run with its own parameters,
scores $\uqQRocha\%$, not distinguishable from it. It does not predict the power: its Bond draw is flat across the surveys, and its held-out power
error of $25.94\%$ is no better than the constant mean (difference $\uqPpBbMean$ points, interval $\uqPpBbMeanlo$ to $\uqPpBbMeanhi$). A line on the
closed-side setting alone has the lowest point estimate of the power error ($12.78\%$, interval $\uqPLinelo$ to $\uqPLinehi$, chosen on the same
surveys); Morrell's size-specific energy with a fitted work index is worse than the line ($38.08\%$), and with a no-load term whose fitted index is
negative in every fold ($15.21\%$) it is, like CrusherBayes ($14.31\%$) and the free-form fit, not distinguishable from the line on ten surveys.
CrusherBayes gives held-out intervals that cover eight and nine of the ten surveys and are $44\%$ narrower than the backbone band on power, about as
wide as the band of the line there, and $7\%$ narrower on throughput. Its posterior contracts the noise and the dependence of the discrepancy on the
setting and on $f_{80}$; it contracts the capacity base only through the prior scale of the discrepancy, which can absorb any change of that base,
and it leaves the classification and breakage scalars uncontracted. A leaky evaluation would have ordered the free-form fit first on both targets and
the bounded residual second on throughput; held out, the bounded residual is not distinguishable from the backbone. The next steps are measurements, not models: the
product size distribution of each survey, which the population balance needs to be calibrated; the crushing work index and the no-load power of the machine,
which would turn Morrell's method into a prediction; and more surveys, which would resolve differences of a few points that ten cannot.
